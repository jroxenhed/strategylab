## 0. PLAIN-ENGLISH SUMMARY (non-expert reader; every term defined inline)

**What we are testing.** When a company's own *insiders* (its officers and directors) buy their own company's stock, they have to publicly disclose each purchase in a filing called a *Form 4*, filed within 2 business days. We measure how *much* insider buying is happening at a company at a given moment — a single **dose number** that goes *up* when more dollars are bought, when more *different* insiders are buying, and *down* when the company is huge (so a $100k buy at a small company counts for more than at a giant). Then we ask the **dose-response** question: do the companies with the *biggest* insider-buying dose go *up by more than their peers* over the following weeks than the companies with the *smallest* dose? "Dose-response" is the drug-trial idea — if a little of a thing helps a little and a lot helps a lot, in a smooth ladder, that ladder is itself strong evidence the thing is real (much harder to fake than a single yes/no cutoff).

**Why a dose instead of a yes/no rule (John's point).** v1 used a hard switch: "≥ 2 insiders, within 5 business days, ≥ $50,000 — else not a cluster." John rightly asked: *what if they bought within 6 days? what if the total was $49,000? shouldn't a big dollar figure count for less at a giant company?* A hard switch makes the whole finding hostage to arbitrary edges. A **continuous dose** sidesteps all three: 6 days vs 5 just shifts a number slightly, $49k vs $50k is a hair of difference not a cliff, and dividing by company size makes the dollars *proportional to market cap* automatically. We still freeze the dose's formula exactly (so we can't tune it after seeing results), but we no longer pretend the world has a clean on/off line in it.

**Where the insider events come from now (R-1b change).** R-1 read each insider purchase by parsing one filing's raw XML at a time. R-1b instead reads the SEC's **bulk quarterly insider-transaction datasets** — the same trades, but delivered as big tab-separated tables ("TSV" = tab-separated values, a plain-text spreadsheet) covering a whole quarter at once. The code that parses them is `form4_ingest.build_form4_dataset_events`. Four properties of this data source were reviewed and accepted and are **binding facts of this study** (stated in §2a). In plain terms: (a) the bulk files round prices to the nearest cent, so a dose can differ from the old XML-path dose by up to ~0.2% — that's a property of the data, not a bug; (b) when an insider files a *correction* of an earlier Form 4 (called a "4/A" amendment), the correction **replaces** the original (we keep the latest accepted version, matched by company + owner + the period the trade covers); (c) when two genuinely-different filings happen to collide on the same matching key, we **keep both and count them** (never silently merge or drop); (d) when we look up which stock a filing belongs to, we trust the **trading symbol the issuer actually filed** if the universe's default symbol would point at a different instrument, and any old-era symbol with no price history simply removes itself from the study and is **counted**, never silently priced as if it had data.

**The dose (one frozen formula).** For each company on each day, the dose is:

> **score = log( 1 + [ total open-market insider purchase dollars over the trailing 21 business days ] ÷ [ the company's market value that day ] ) × ( 1 + 0.5 × [ number of *different* insiders who bought in that window ] )**

In words: the **first part** is how big the buying was *relative to the company's size* (a $1M buy at a $50M company is a much bigger dose than at a $50B company — this is John's "proportional to market cap"). The `log(1+…)` just keeps one freakishly large buy from dominating everything. The **second part** multiplies the dose up when *more separate people* are buying (two insiders buying is more convincing than one buying twice). Every constant in this formula (the 21-day window, the 0.5 weight on insider count, the log shape) is **frozen here** with a one-line reason, and the whole formula is *hashed like a password* — once approved we cannot nudge any number to chase a result.

**The clock.** *Event-time*: an event is "a fresh insider open-market purchase prints for this company," and we enter the trade at the **next trading day's opening price** after the latest Form 4 in the dose window became public — never on the filing day itself, so we never pretend to trade on information before it was public.

**The comparison + the headline question.** For each event we compute the stock's forward return and subtract the *same-day median return of all other tradeable stocks* (the "universe median" — a fair "what did an average stock do over the same stretch" benchmark). **R-1b reads that universe median from a frozen snapshot** (see next paragraph). The leftover is the *universe excess return*. We then **sort every event into five buckets by its dose** (lowest fifth, …, highest fifth — "quintiles"), *separately within each calendar year* so we're not fooled by good-vs-bad eras. The **headline win** is a *dose-response ladder*: the **top-dose fifth must beat the bottom-dose fifth** at 63 trading days (~3 months) with statistical confidence, AND the five buckets must **trend the right way** (bigger dose → bigger excess, tested as a rank-correlation). A smooth ladder is the finding; a single lucky bucket is not.

**The frozen benchmark snapshot (R-1b change).** Every universe-median (and sector-peer-median) forward return in R-1b is read from one permanently-pinned file, the **frozen returns matrix** (artifact `backend/data/universe_matrix.parquet`), pinned by the exact phrase **"universe medians from matrix build 2026-06-07."** Why: the live price feed lets the data vendor quietly **re-adjust history** (for splits, dividends) between cache refreshes, so the "median stock's return" drifts by tiny amounts depending on the day you happened to fetch it — which means two runs of the same study could disagree at the third decimal place for no real reason. Freezing one vintage of the matrix nails the benchmark down forever, so the study is **exactly reproducible**. The matrix loader **refuses to load a partial file**; if the matrix's coverage sidecar (a small companion file recording how complete the build was) shows a nonzero `no_frame_count` (the number of tickers the build couldn't find price frames for), we **rebuild and compare before using it** rather than trusting a partial snapshot.

**The two benchmarks (John's solar-bucket point).** Subtracting "all other stocks" has a blind spot John flagged: *if insiders at a solar company buy and the whole solar sector rips, did the stock beat its herd or just ride it?* Beating the *average stock* is easy if your whole industry is on fire — you'd look like a genius for simply owning a solar name in a solar bull run. So v3 measures excess **two ways for every event**: (a) **universe excess** — versus the average of *all* tradeable stocks (this is what *following the signal actually earns you*, so it stays the headline); and (b) **peer excess** — versus the average of only the stocks *in the same industry bucket* (its "sector peers," identified by a standard government industry code called **SIC** — solar names share one code, semiconductors another, memory-chip makers another). Peer excess answers the harder question: did this stock beat *its own herd*, or just ride the herd up? Both numbers are frozen and reported. If the signal beats the broad market but does **not** beat its own sector peers, we don't call it a clean stock-picking edge — we call it **CONFIRMED-SECTOR-CARRIED** (real, but it's riding sector waves), and any trading playbook built on it must cap how many same-sector names it holds at once (so you're not secretly making one big solar bet dressed up as five independent picks).

**The sector-peer fallback (when a bucket is too small).** Some industry buckets are thin — on a given day there may be only a handful of floor-passing names sharing the exact 3-digit industry code. When fewer than 8 sector peers exist, we widen to the broader 2-digit industry code; if that's *still* under 8, we fall back to the universe median (and flag that event as "peer_fallback"). If more than 40% of events end up falling back, the peer lens is too sparse to trust and is declared **UNDERPOWERED** for that run — it then draws no weight in the verdict (we say so explicitly).

**The third lens — market weather (John's regime-breakdown ask).** Beyond *when* (the era lens) and *which industry* (the sector lens), there's *what the market was doing* — the **regime**, our shorthand for the **market weather** measured purely from price: is the broad market in an uptrend or downtrend (trend), calm or choppy (volatility), and are most stocks participating or just a few (breadth)? A frozen classifier reads those three things every day and labels the day **RISK_ON** (calm uptrend, broad participation), **NEUTRAL** (in-between), **RISK_OFF** (high choppiness, no clean uptrend), or **STRESS** (a high-vol crash with broad weakness). Each trade gets stamped with the weather on its entry day, and the verdict **reports the headline result inside each weather bucket** — does the insider-dose edge show up in calm markets, choppy markets, both? This is a **reporting lens only: it adds no new pass/fail bar** and cannot, by itself, fail the experiment. It does add **one gentle annotation**: if the edge shows up in *only one* of the three usable weather states (RISK_ON, NEUTRAL, or RISK_OFF) and is flat-or-negative in the other two — each judged on at least 15 trades, because thinner buckets aren't trustworthy — we *flag* it **REGIME-CARRIED** ("this edge depends on the weather"). Crucially, unlike the sector flag, this does **not** downgrade the verdict: a weather-dependent edge is still a *complete, real* edge, because the weather is something you can *see in real time* — you simply lean into the trade when the weather is right and ease off when it isn't. The only thing it changes downstream is that the trading playbook's *conviction* (how hard it bets) must rise and fall with the current weather. The **STRESS** bucket (the crash-day weather) is *reported but never allowed to carry a conclusion*: STRESS days happen only about **6 days per decade**, and 6 days a decade is simply not enough to learn anything from — so any STRESS number is labelled "not evidence" regardless of how it looks.

**The wiggle test (John's "$49k", institutionalized).** Because the dose has frozen constants, we are *required* to re-run the headline comparison with the buying-window nudged to **20 or 22 business days** (±1) and the implicit dollar/size floor nudged **±20%**, and check that the answer *keeps the same sign*. We do NOT require it to stay statistically significant at every wiggle — only that the direction doesn't flip. If nudging a frozen knob by a hair flips the sign, the finding is fragile and the verdict is **capped at WEAKENED** no matter how good the headline number looked. This is a *frozen robustness check*, decided now — it is the opposite of a search for the best setting.

**The power check (so we don't "confirm" something the test was too weak to see).** Before the sealed confirm run, the harness reports the *smallest edge it could reliably detect* (the "MDE"). If that's bigger than the smallest edge worth trading on a ~$10k account after costs, we **abort rather than run an underpowered confirm**. (This is the gate R-1 stopped at; R-1b re-runs it cleanly on the new data source.)

**What the verdicts mean.** CONFIRMED = the dose-response ladder held up out-of-sample (top fifth beat bottom fifth, the trend was monotone). WEAKENED = it showed in explore but the confirm didn't clear the bar, *or* the wiggle test flipped a sign. REVERSED = the dose ladder ran *backwards* (more insider buying → *worse* returns) with confidence. UNTESTABLE = too few events or too weak a test. Whatever the result, it updates the Form-4 corollary of the program's "price leads, filings trail" axiom *only* at the program's Decision Gate, never by this experiment alone.

## 2. EXACT EVENT DEFINITION + FROZEN CONTINUOUS SCORE (no grid)

**Source events (R-1b: dataset-ingest layer).** Form 4 / 4/A events come from the **Form 4 dataset-ingest layer** — `backend/research/form4_ingest.build_form4_dataset_events(quarters=…, dedup_amendments=True)` — which parses the SEC's quarterly Insider Transactions Data Set ZIPs (`backend/data/turnaround/edgar_cache/form4_datasets/`) into `EventRecord`-compatible streams. **This replaces R-1's per-filing XML path.** Open-market code-P purchases (transaction code **P** with acquired/disposed code **A**) and distinct reporting-owner CIKs are identified by the ingest layer using the same economic definitions R-1 used; per-transaction dollar value = `transactionShares × transactionPricePerShare` parsed from the dataset table; if price-per-share is absent on a qualifying P transaction, that transaction contributes **$0** to the dollar total (conservative — never imputed upward). **No live EDGAR fetch is performed by this experiment for outcomes** — it reads only the cached quarterly datasets on disk (the ingest's optional `fetch_missing` path is used only to backfill missing acceptance-index pages, never to fetch outcomes).

**Four binding properties of the dataset-ingest source (reviewed and accepted; facts of this study, not errors).**
- **(a) 2-decimal price rounding.** The TSV dataset rounds `transactionPricePerShare` to 2 decimal places, so a dose value can differ from the old XML-path dose by up to ~**0.2%**. This is a **data-source property, not an error** — it is below the perturbation band's ±20% floor wiggle and far below any effect the §3a MDE gate could resolve.
- **(b) 4/A amendments SUPERSEDE their originals.** A 4/A is a corrected refiling of an earlier Form 4. Amendments are deduplicated **globally after all quarters are parsed** (cross-quarter pairs exist), keyed by **(issuer CIK, owner CIK, period-of-report)**; when a '4' and a '4/A' share a key, **only the latest acceptanceDateTime is kept** (tie-break: higher accession number). **`dedup_amendments=True` is pinned** for this study.
- **(c) Ambiguous all-original key collisions are KEPT and counted.** When two genuinely-distinct all-original filings collide on the same (issuer, owner, period) key, they are **NEVER silently merged or dropped** — both are kept and the collision is counted (recorded in ingest meta, surfaced in the §7 survivorship/coverage line).
- **(d) Ticker resolution trusts the issuer's filed trading symbol.** The CIK→ticker join trusts the **issuer's filed trading symbol** when the universe map's primary symbol would point at a different instrument. Era-symbols with no price frame **self-exclude downstream and are counted** (they surface as `events_no_price_data` / `score_undefined`, §7), **never silently priced** as if they had data.

### 2a. What counts as an EVENT (when the clock starts)

An **event** = the public arrival of a fresh **open-market code-P purchase** Form 4 on a company that did not already have a same-ticker event inside the de-dup window (§6). Concretely: scanning the cached dataset stream in time order, each company-day on which a new qualifying P-purchase Form 4 is *accepted* opens one candidate event, timestamped at that filing's `acceptanceDateTime` (the ingest layer's `filingDate + 16:01 ET` fallback is used only when acceptanceDateTime is absent; fallback count recorded). The §6 same-ticker de-dup (window = **30 calendar days**, §6) then collapses bursts so one wave of buying = one degree of freedom.

The event is **not gated on a minimum cluster** — *every* qualifying P-purchase arrival is an event. The "how much of a cluster is this" question is answered **entirely by the continuous score** (§2b), which every event carries on its `EventRecord.payload` (the harness forwards payload verbatim into the outcome table, so the dose travels with the event into the analysis). This is the core v2 change: there is no binary "is it a cluster" cut; there is a continuous dose, and the hypothesis is about the *gradient* of forward excess across that dose (§4).

**Midnight-UTC acceptance timestamps (F359, 2026-06-07 — no exclusion, provenance flag only).** Some dataset acceptance timestamps land at ~**midnight UTC**. Every affected filing in the study population was checked against the SEC website (F359): all are **genuine evening acceptances** (7–8pm New York time, which equals midnight UTC), the **event date is unaffected**, and **no exclusion is applied**. Each such event carries an `adt_midnight_utc = True` provenance flag (set by the ingest layer) so the population is auditable; the flag is descriptive only and changes no dose, no entry date, and no verdict bar.

### 2b. THE FROZEN CONTINUOUS SCORE (the dose — hashed like a threshold)

For an event on ticker T whose latest qualifying Form 4 was accepted on date `d`, define a **trailing dose window** of **W = 21 business days** ending at `d` (inclusive). Let, over all qualifying open-market **code-P** purchases on T whose Form 4 acceptance falls in that window:

- `D` = **total purchase dollars** = Σ (`transactionShares × transactionPricePerShare`) across those P transactions (missing price ⇒ that transaction = $0; dataset prices are 2-decimal-rounded per §2(a)).
- `k` = **count of distinct reporting-owner CIKs** that contributed ≥ 1 qualifying P purchase in the window (the "how many *different* insiders" number).
- `MC(T, d)` = **point-in-time market cap** = `edgar.get_shares_outstanding(cik(T), as_of=d)` (most-recent `CommonStockSharesOutstanding` filed on or before `d`, point-in-time) **×** the **cached daily close of T on the last trading day ≤ `d`** (the same price vintage the frozen returns matrix uses, §2c). If shares-outstanding is unavailable (`None`) or the cached close is missing, the market cap is undefined and the event is **counted as `score_undefined` and excluded from the dose-response analysis** (never imputed — ADV-02 style, recorded in meta).

Then the **FROZEN SCORE** is:

> **score = log1p( D / MC(T, d) ) · ( 1 + β · k )**, with **β = 0.5**.

Equivalently `score = math.log1p(D / MC) * (1 + 0.5 * k)`. The **dollar-intensity ratio** `D / MC` is John's "proportional to market cap" made literal; `log1p` tames a single outlier purchase; the `(1 + 0.5·k)` factor is the **distinct-insider multiplier** (a frozen multiplier, per the revision spec — chosen over an additive term so that, with zero dollars, the score is zero regardless of headcount: dose requires *dollars*, and headcount only *amplifies* a real-dollar dose).

**Every constant FROZEN here with one-line rationale (each hashed into the config fingerprint):**

| Constant | Frozen value | One-line rationale (stated, not tuned) |
|---|---|---|
| **Trailing dose window W** | **21 business days** | One trading month — long enough to gather a genuine multi-insider wave and absorb the ≤2-bday Form-4 filing lag, short enough that the buys plausibly share one information event. (Replaces v1's hard "5-business-day cluster window"; the dose now degrades smoothly across the window edge rather than gating on it — directly answers "what if 6 days not 5?".) |
| **Dollar-intensity ratio** | **D / MC** (dollars ÷ point-in-time market cap) | Makes the dose *proportional to company size* (John's question): identical dollars are a larger dose at a smaller company. MC = point-in-time shares (`get_shares_outstanding`) × cached close — both already on disk, no live fetch. |
| **Shape transform** | **log1p(·)** (= ln(1+x)) | Diminishing returns: prevents one gigantic buy (or a tiny-float micro-cap) from swamping the ranking; `log1p` is finite at 0 (a zero-dollar window scores 0). |
| **Distinct-insider weight β** | **0.5** (multiplicative: `1 + 0.5·k`) | More *separate* insiders is more convincing than one insider buying repeatedly; 0.5 makes each additional distinct insider add 50% of the base dollar-dose — a modest, fixed amplification, not a tuned optimum. Multiplicative so dose=0 when dollars=0 regardless of k. |
| **Distinct-insider count k** | **distinct reporting-owner CIKs with a P-buy in W** | One-insider-one-vote (title-agnostic), same primitive as v1's insider count; officer/director title is *recorded* descriptively but carries no weight (avoids a tunable knob). |
| **10b5-1 exclusion** | **Exclude when machine-identifiable** | Pre-scheduled 10b5-1 trades carry no timing signal; a transaction flagged `rule10b5-1` (form-level REMARKS + all-footnote scan, plus the row-level SECURITY_TITLE scan the ingest layer performs) is dropped from *both* `D` and `k`. Non-identifiable = retained (we do not guess); the fraction excluded is recorded per window. |

**Why no binary gate survives.** v1's three binary cuts (≥2 insiders, ≤5 days, ≥$50k) are *all* dissolved into the score: insider count → the `k` multiplier, the 5-day window → the smooth 21-bday trailing window, the $50k floor → the continuous `D/MC` ratio (no floor at all in the primary; a soft floor is only *perturbed* in §3b as a robustness check, never as a gate). **The hypothesis is no longer "do clusters beat peers" but "does forward excess rise monotonically with the dose" (§4).** The score is the single frozen instrument; its formula and every constant are part of the config hash, so post-hoc tuning is structurally impossible without minting a new experiment (§10).

**Entry semantics (harness default, frozen).** `entry_lag_days = 1`: entry is the **Open of the first trading day after the event's ET date** (`event_study.py` `_entry_date_from_event_ts`, Fork A / Option 1). No same-day entry (the harness's ADV-09 contamination case).

**Direction:** **long** for all events (§7 cost model).

**Universe floors:** the harness's standard floors (`research.universe_floors.floor_status`, evaluated point-in-time at the **event date**, ADV-01 — never the entry bar). Below-floor / corrupt-frame events are **counted, never silently dropped** (ADV-02). The universe median benchmark is computed over all floor-passing symbols alive on the same entry date, the pick excluded (Fork B), **read from the frozen returns matrix** (§2c).

### 2c. DUAL BENCHMARK — UNIVERSE EXCESS + SECTOR-PEER EXCESS (both frozen; F349 dependency) + FROZEN RETURNS-MATRIX PIN (R-1b)

Per v3, **every event carries TWO frozen excess measures at every horizon**, computed identically except for the peer set the median is taken over. Both travel on the outcome row; neither is chosen post-hoc.

- **(a) Universe excess (PRIMARY, unchanged from v2).** `excess_univ = pick forward return − same-entry-date floor-passing **universe** median forward return` (picks excluded; `event_study._compute_universe_median`, Fork B). This measures the **tradeable edge** — what following the signal earns against the broad market — and remains the **primary verdict instrument** (§4 H1/H1b/H2 bars are read on universe excess).
- **(b) Peer excess (MANDATORY CO-REGISTERED LENS, new).** `excess_peer = pick forward return − same-entry-date **sector-peer** median forward return`, where the sector-peer set is the floor-passing names that, on the entry date, share the event ticker's **3-digit SIC prefix** (pick excluded, same Fork-B / ADV-01 / ADV-03 construction as the universe median — same floor enforcement, same terminal-exit handling). This measures whether the dose beat the stock's **own industry herd** rather than merely riding a sector wave (John's solar-bucket point).

**FROZEN RETURNS-MATRIX PIN (R-1b — binding).** All universe-median **and** sector-peer-median forward returns are read from the **frozen universe-returns matrix**, pinned by the exact phrase **"universe medians from matrix build 2026-06-07"** (artifact `backend/data/universe_matrix.parquet`, with its metadata/coverage sidecar `_meta.json` inside the parquet directory). **Rationale (binding to state):** the live fetch path lets the price vendor **retroactively re-adjust history** (split/dividend re-statement) between cache refreshes, so the median forward returns drift microscopically across fetch dates; the frozen matrix freezes **one vintage permanently** so the study is **exactly reproducible** bit-for-bit. **The matrix loader refuses partial artifacts** (it raises rather than silently load an incomplete snapshot); and a **nonzero `no_frame_count` in the artifact's coverage sidecar** (`ticker_coverage.no_frame_count` — tickers for which the build found no price frame) means **rebuild-and-compare before use** — a nonzero count signals the matrix is not a clean full snapshot and must be regenerated and diffed before the study reads any median from it. The matrix vintage (build 2026-06-07) is part of the config fingerprint exactly like a frozen constant.

**SIC source (config/identity data, no outcomes).** Each issuer's SIC is the top-level `"sic"` string in its EDGAR submissions JSON (`backend/data/turnaround/edgar_cache/submissions/<cik>.json`; e.g. AMD → `"sic": "3674"`, "Semiconductors & Related Devices"). The **3-digit prefix** is the first 3 characters of that 4-digit code (`"3674" → "367"`). SIC is resolved point-in-time by issuer CIK (an issuer's SIC is stable identity data, not an outcome); the mapping `ticker → CIK → sic` reuses the same CIK resolution the dose already uses (`cik(T)`).

**FROZEN FALLBACK LADDER (decided now, no post-hoc additions).** For each event, the sector-peer set is built on the entry date by this exact ladder, and the **first rung yielding ≥ 8 floor-passing peers (pick excluded) wins**:
1. **3-digit SIC prefix** (e.g. `367`). If the floor-passing peer count on the entry date is **≥ 8**, use this set.
2. **Else 2-digit SIC prefix** (e.g. `36`). If that yields **≥ 8** floor-passing peers, use this set; the event is flagged **`peer_fallback = "2digit"`**.
3. **Else the full universe** (the §2b universe-median set). The event is flagged **`peer_fallback = "universe"`** — for such an event peer excess equals universe excess by construction (it contributes no independent peer-lens information).

The `< 8 peers` rung-advance threshold is frozen here (8 ≈ the minimum at which a same-date sector median is not dominated by a single name; chosen on the same small-sample reasoning as the quintile-occupancy floors, not tuned). Per-event the chosen rung (`peer_rung ∈ {"3digit","2digit","universe"}`), the peer count, and the `peer_fallback` flag are recorded on the outcome row. Both the universe-median set and every peer-median set are read from the same frozen matrix vintage above.

**PEER-FALLBACK RATE + UNDERPOWERED-LENS RULE (frozen, reported in meta).** The harness records, per window, the **peer-fallback rate** = fraction of valid events whose `peer_rung != "3digit"` (i.e. that did not get a clean 3-digit sector peer set), broken out as `meta["peer_benchmark"] = {frac_3digit, frac_2digit, frac_universe, frac_fallback}`. **Binding rule:** if the **peer-fallback rate exceeds 40%** of valid events in a window, the **peer lens is declared UNDERPOWERED for that run and draws NO verdict weight** — the §4 sector-carried cap does NOT fire from an underpowered peer lens (a mostly-fallen-back peer excess is mostly universe excess and cannot honestly contradict it). The verdict states this explicitly ("peer lens UNDERPOWERED, fallback rate = ⟨value⟩% > 40%; sector-carried cap not evaluated") and the universe-excess verdict stands on its own. Events with no resolvable SIC (missing submissions record or empty `sic`) are counted (`sic_unresolved`) and treated as rung-3 (universe) fallbacks.

## 3. CLOCK + MDE STATEMENT + PERTURBATION BAND (frozen)

### 3a. Clock + MDE (mandatory power gate)

**Clock.** Event-time, `entry_lag_days = 1` (next-trading-open after acceptanceDateTime → ET → next trading day), exactly as `event_study.py` documents as its default. acceptanceDateTime is the primary timestamp; the `filingDate + 16:01 ET` fallback is used only when acceptanceDateTime is absent, and the count of fallback timestamps is recorded (`acceptance_dt_fallbacks` in meta). (R-1b note: this is the gate at which R-1 aborted; R-1b re-evaluates it cleanly on the dataset-ingest event stream and the frozen matrix.)

**MDE self-report is MANDATORY and runs BEFORE confirm (binding).**
- The harness self-reports the **MDE — minimum detectable effect** = the smallest true mean excess detectable at **80% power, two-sided α = 0.05** (`outcome_table.minimum_detectable_effect`, `z_α=1.96`, `z_power=0.842`), per horizon on both the cohort-relative **excess** and the **raw absolute** return (ADV-07), printed by `print_study_report`.
- **For the dose-response primary**, the MDE that gates the experiment is computed on the **Q5−Q1 difference distribution** at 63td on the explore stream — i.e. the smallest top-minus-bottom-quintile mean-excess gap the test could resolve at 80% power, using the pooled per-quintile n and σ. (The single-sample whole-stream MDE is also reported, but the *gate* is on the Q5−Q1 contrast because that is the headline.) Procedure: run the harness on the **explore event stream first**, compute the per-year quintile assignment from the frozen score (§4), read the explore Q5−Q1 63td MDE. **The confirm window is NOT touched until this is read and checked against the abort rule.**

**MDE ABORT RULE (frozen, decided now — cannot be relaxed post-hoc).**
- **Smallest economically meaningful edge for a ~$10k account after costs.** Per repo cost conventions the modeled cost is **2 bps/leg slippage + $0 commission** (§7), ~**4 bps round-trip ≈ 0.04 percentage points (pp)** of drag per trade. We set the **smallest economically meaningful 63td Q5−Q1 excess gap = 1.0 percentage point** (≈ 25× the round-trip drag; a sub-1pp three-month top-minus-bottom-dose gap is below what a ~$10k retail account can reliably harvest after costs and sizing friction). Fixed here on cost reasoning, not on any observed effect size.
- **The rule:** if the explore-window **63td Q5−Q1 MDE > 1.0 pp**, the design is **underpowered to detect even the smallest dose-response gap worth trading**, and the experiment **ABORTS before the confirm run**. Verdict = **UNTESTABLE — underpowered (Q5−Q1 MDE = ⟨value⟩ pp > 1.0 pp floor)**; confirm stays sealed; the Form-4 corollary's evidence status is left unchanged.
- If `Q5−Q1 MDE ≤ 1.0 pp` at 63td on explore, the confirm proceeds under §4/§8.
- The MDE (Q5−Q1, whole-stream excess, and raw) at all three horizons is **reported regardless**, alongside `n_explore_valid` and the per-quintile event counts.

### 3b. PRE-REGISTERED PERTURBATION BAND (John's "$49k", institutionalized — frozen)

The charter **mandates** re-running the §4 primary comparison (the Q5−Q1 63td excess gap and the monotonicity trend) under a small, frozen band of perturbations to the score's constants, and requires **sign stability** — *not* significance at every setting. This is a **frozen robustness check, NOT a search**: its only output is a per-perturbation sign of the Q5−Q1 gap (and of the Spearman trend), and the binary verdict-capping flag below. No perturbation result is ever selected as "the" answer; the frozen-constant result of §2b remains the headline.

**The frozen perturbation set (decided now, no additions post-hoc):**

| Perturbation | Settings re-run | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Window W ± 1 business day** | W ∈ {**20, 21, 22**} business days | John's "what if 6 days not 5?" — does a one-bday nudge of the dose window flip the dose-response sign? |
| **Score-floor ± 20%** | apply a soft minimum-dollar floor `F` to `D` before the ratio, at **F ∈ {0 (frozen primary), $40k, $60k}** (i.e. a $50k reference floor perturbed ±20%; events with `D < F` get `D` clamped to 0 for that perturbation only) | John's "what if the sum is $49k?" — does where we put the dollar floor flip the sign? (The frozen primary has **no** floor, F=0; the ±20% band is the robustness probe around the v1 $50k notion.) |

For **each** perturbation setting the harness re-derives per-year quintiles from the perturbed score and recomputes the Q5−Q1 63td mean-excess gap and the Spearman quintile-trend sign. The band is run on **both** explore (reported) and, if the experiment reaches it, confirm (the confirm band is part of the single sealed touch).

**SIGN-STABILITY REQUIREMENT (frozen, verdict-capping):**
- The requirement is **sign stability of the Q5−Q1 gap AND the monotonic trend across the *entire* band** — every setting in {W∈{20,21,22}} × {F∈{0,40k,60k}} must show the **same sign** as the frozen-constant primary (positive gap, positive trend). Statistical significance is **not** required at every band setting (small per-bucket n makes that too strict); only the **direction** must hold.
- **A sign flip anywhere in the band → the verdict is CAPPED at WEAKENED**, regardless of how significant the headline (frozen-constant) p-value is. A finding that depends on whether the window is 20 vs 22 days, or the floor is $40k vs $60k, is fragile by John's own standard and is not promotable to CONFIRMED.
- The full band table (sign of Q5−Q1 gap + Spearman sign per setting) is **reported in the verdict** so the cap is auditable. This is a pre-registered branch, not a post-hoc carve-out.

## 4. HYPOTHESES (H1 dose-response / H1b trend / H2 horizon) + TEST (frozen before any outcome data)

**Horizons.** Primary = **63 trading days (~3 months)**. Secondaries (descriptive, pre-registered, NOT separately alpha-scanned) = **21 td** and **126 td** (`V2_HORIZONS_TRADING_DAYS = (21, 63, 126)`).

**Outcome metric.** Per-event cohort-relative **universe excess** = `pick Open-anchored forward return − same-entry-date universe-median forward return` at the matched horizon (`event_study.py` `_forward_return_terminal` + `_compute_universe_median`, ADV-03, with the universe median read from the frozen matrix build 2026-06-07, §2c), NET of the §7 cost model. **This is the PRIMARY metric — all §4 H1/H1b/H2 bars below are read on universe excess.** Per §2c, every event ALSO carries a co-registered **peer excess** (same forward return minus the same-entry-date 3-digit-SIC sector-peer median from the same frozen matrix, fallback ladder per §2c); the peer lens is read on the **same comparisons** as a mandatory co-registered check that can only *cap* (never lift) the verdict (§4 sector-carried rule, §9).

**Quintile construction (frozen).** Within **each calendar year** of the window (explore: 2015…2020; confirm: 2021…2024), rank that year's valid events by the §2b frozen score and split into **5 equal-count quintiles** (Q1 = lowest dose … Q5 = highest dose). **Within-year** stratification removes era-mix confounds (a year that was simply good for everything cannot masquerade as a dose effect). Quintile assignment is on the de-duplicated event stream (§6). Events with `score_undefined` (missing MC) are excluded from quintiling and counted. The per-year-per-quintile mean 63td excess is the analysis unit; the window-level quintile mean pools across years within the window.

**Primary test machinery.** Harness **moving-block bootstrap** (Künsch MBB, `_block_bootstrap_pvalue`), `n_boot = 999`, SEED 20260606, block size density-derived per horizon (`_block_size_for_horizon`), capped at `n//2`. **Cross-check** = Newey–West HAC t-test (`_nw_ttest_pvalue`). For the Q5−Q1 contrast the bootstrap resamples the **difference** of the two quintiles' per-event excesses (block-resampled to respect overlap). The bootstrap and NW p-values must **agree within 0.10** at the primary horizon; a larger gap is flagged as an event-density / block-size caveat.

### H1 — PRIMARY: DOSE-RESPONSE (top-dose quintile beats bottom-dose quintile at 63td).
- **Prediction:** the **top-quintile (Q5) mean 63td excess > the bottom-quintile (Q1) mean 63td excess** — i.e. the Q5−Q1 difference is **> 0**. (More insider-buying dose → more forward excess.)
- **CONFIRMED bar (ALL required, on the sealed confirm window 2021–2024):**
  1. **Power gate passed** (§3a: explore 63td Q5−Q1 MDE ≤ 1.0 pp — else UNTESTABLE, no confirm).
  2. **Occupancy:** confirm window has **≥ 3 of the 4 confirm years** (2021/2022/2023/2024) populated with ≥ 1 valid event in **both** Q1 and Q5, AND `n_confirm_valid ≥ 25` total valid 63td events (a higher floor than v1's 15 because the design now needs both tails populated for the contrast — 25 keeps ≥ ~5 per quintile; below 25 → UNTESTABLE-confirm).
  3. **Point estimate:** confirm 63td **Q5−Q1 mean excess > 0** (correct direction).
  4. **CI excludes zero:** the confirm 63td seeded block-bootstrap test on the **Q5−Q1 difference** rejects H0 at the per-comparison α (§5) with the difference on the **positive** side (bootstrap CI lower bound on Q5−Q1 > 0).
  5. **Perturbation sign-stability holds (§3b):** the confirm Q5−Q1 gap sign is **stable across the entire perturbation band** (W∈{20,21,22} × F∈{0,40k,60k}). A flip caps the verdict at WEAKENED (§3b, §9).
- **Explore precondition for *reaching* confirm:** confirm is touched ONLY if the **explore** window shows a **positive Q5−Q1 63td gap** AND explore perturbation sign-stability (§3b) holds AND **H1b (below) trends the right way in explore**. Otherwise the experiment stops at explore with verdict **WEAKENED-IN-EXPLORE / not advanced to confirm**; confirm stays sealed.

### H1b — DOSE-RESPONSE MONOTONICITY (the whole ladder trends, not just the endpoints).
- **Prediction:** across the five quintile means (Q1…Q5) of 63td excess, **excess rises with dose** — a monotone-increasing ladder.
- **Frozen test:** **one-sided Spearman rank correlation** between the **quintile index (1…5)** and the **per-quintile mean 63td excess**, ρ_s, tested one-sided (Hₐ: ρ_s > 0) at the §5 α. (Spearman, not Pearson, because we care only that the buckets *order* correctly, not their exact spacing — robust to the dose's nonlinear scale.) A complementary **per-year** Spearman is reported (the within-year ρ_s for each year), and the **fraction of years with ρ_s > 0** is recorded as a consistency read.
- **H1b bar:** on the confirm window, ρ_s > 0 and **one-sided-rejected** at the §5 α. H1b is **alpha-bearing** (it is the dose-response claim) and is part of the FDR family (§5). H1 (Q5−Q1 endpoints) and H1b (full-ladder trend) are **both** required for CONFIRMED — endpoints could separate while the middle is noise; the Spearman insists the *gradient* is real.

### H2 — TRADEABLE-THRESHOLD CANDIDATE (subordinate, the Radar-desk product).
- This is the **clearly-subordinate** secondary: even though the *finding* is dose-response, the *tradeable product* is a top-quintile-only long basket (you cannot trade a correlation, you trade the high-dose names). **Prediction:** the **Q5 (top-dose) absolute mean 63td excess > 0** on the confirm window (not just relative to Q1).
- **H2 bar:** confirm Q5 mean 63td excess > 0 with the seeded block-bootstrap rejecting H0 on the positive side at the §5 α. H2 **HOLDS** iff so. H2 is **subordinate**: it does NOT gate H1/H1b, and it is alpha-bearing only for the "top-quintile is a tradeable Radar candidate" wording. If H1/H1b CONFIRM but H2 fails (the gradient is real but the top bucket alone doesn't clear zero net), the verdict is CONFIRMED-as-dose-response with a note that *the tradeable top-quintile product is not yet established*.

### H3 — HORIZON CONSISTENCY (descriptive, not gating, not alpha-bearing).
- Report the Q5−Q1 gap and Spearman trend at 21td and 126td alongside 63td. Descriptive consistency only; not separately alpha-scanned.

### PEER LENS — MANDATORY CO-REGISTERED SECTOR-CARRIED CHECK (frozen, verdict-capping; NOT a separate alpha draw).

The peer excess (§2c) is read on the **same primary comparison** as H1 — the **Q5−Q1 63td gap, but computed on peer excess** — on the confirm window. It is **not** a new alpha-bearing hypothesis and is **not** a member of the FDR family (§5): it cannot *lift* a verdict, draw alpha, or rescue a failed universe-excess test. It can only **cap** a passing one. Its single binding output:

- **Frozen sector-carried bar (R-1b):** the **peer-excess Q5−Q1 63td point estimate must be > 0** (strictly positive; a peer-excess Q5−Q1 point estimate **≤ 0** is the "no positive peer effect" condition). Significance is **not** required on the peer lens — only the **point-estimate sign** — because the peer cohorts are thinner (sector buckets are smaller than the universe) and demanding significance there would be too strict; a non-positive point estimate is nonetheless a clean statement that the dose did **not** beat its own sector herd.
- **THE BINDING RULE.** IF the universe-excess H1 (and H1b) **pass** to CONFIRMED (§4/§9) BUT the **peer-excess Q5−Q1 63td point estimate ≤ 0** on the confirm window, THEN the verdict is **CAPPED at CONFIRMED-SECTOR-CARRIED** (§9) — the dose-response edge is real against the broad market but does **not** beat the event's own industry peers, i.e. it rides sector waves. Any resulting Radar playbook **MUST carry a sector-concentration limit** (see §9 for the exact requirement).
- **Underpowered-lens override (§2c).** If the peer-fallback rate > 40% on the confirm window, the peer lens is **UNDERPOWERED** and this rule **does not fire** — the universe-excess verdict stands uncapped, and the verdict states the peer lens could not be evaluated. (A mostly-fallen-back peer excess is mostly universe excess and cannot honestly contradict it.)
- **Reporting (binding):** the confirm verdict reports the peer-excess Q5−Q1 63td point estimate (and its sign), the peer-excess Spearman trend sign, and the `meta["peer_benchmark"]` fallback breakdown + `meta["sic_coverage"]`, so the cap (or the underpowered-lens skip) is auditable from the verdict alone.

### REGIME LENS — MANDATORY CO-REGISTERED MARKET-WEATHER BREAKDOWN (frozen, REPORTING-ONLY; NO pass/fail bar; v4).

The regime tag (§2e) is read on the **same primary comparison** as H1 — the **Q5−Q1 63td universe-excess gap, broken out by the entry-date regime state** — on the confirm window. It is **not** a new alpha-bearing hypothesis, is **not** a member of the FDR family (§5), and **adds NO pass/fail bar**: it cannot abort the experiment, cannot demote a CONFIRMED verdict below CONFIRMED, and cannot lift or rescue a failed one. Its outputs are exactly two — a per-regime breakdown (reported) and one symmetric qualifier flag (annotation only):

- **Per-regime breakdown (reported, binding).** The confirm verdict reports, for each of {RISK_ON, NEUTRAL, RISK_OFF, STRESS}, the Q5−Q1 63td universe-excess point estimate + sign + the per-regime valid-event count, plus `meta["regime_coverage"]`. STRESS is reported but **labelled non-evidential** (§2e) and never cited as support or contradiction.
- **REGIME-CARRIED qualifier (frozen, symmetric with sector-carried but NON-DEMOTING).** Consider only the **evidential** regime cells {RISK_ON, NEUTRAL, RISK_OFF}, and only those with **≥ 15 valid events** (cells below 15 are non-evidential and **cannot trigger or block the flag** — they are simply omitted from the rule). **IF** the Q5−Q1 63td universe-excess point estimate is **positive in exactly ONE** of those evidential, ≥15-event cells and **≤ 0 in each of the others** (that also have ≥15 events), **THEN the verdict carries the flag REGIME-CARRIED** — the edge is concentrated in a single market-weather regime.
- **Why REGIME-CARRIED ANNOTATES but does NOT demote (the asymmetry with sector-carried, frozen rationale).** Sector-carried demotes (to CONFIRMED-SECTOR-CARRIED) because an edge that merely rides industry waves is an *attribution* problem you cannot act on cleanly. A regime-carried edge is different: **the regime is observable in real time** (the classifier reads today's weather from today's price), so a weather-dependent edge is still a **complete, actionable edge** — you simply lean in when the regime is favourable. REGIME-CARRIED is therefore a **qualifier, NOT a cap**: a CONFIRMED verdict that is also REGIME-CARRIED **remains CONFIRMED** (it is annotated, not downgraded — there is no "CONFIRMED-REGIME-CARRIED" demotion level).
- **Downstream consequence (binding).** A REGIME-CARRIED playbook's **Radar conviction MUST scale with the current regime state** — full conviction in the regime where the edge lives, reduced (or zero) conviction in the regimes where the Q5−Q1 gap was ≤0. This sizing rule is the entire actionable content of the flag and MUST be stated in any Radar playbook derived from a REGIME-CARRIED verdict.
- **Reporting (binding):** the confirm verdict states the per-regime Q5−Q1 63td gaps + signs + counts, whether the REGIME-CARRIED flag fired (and if so, in which single regime the edge lives), and the regime-coverage figure — so the annotation is auditable from the verdict alone.

**UNTESTABLE rule (encoded up front):** explore 63td Q5−Q1 MDE > 1.0 pp → UNTESTABLE-underpowered (no confirm). Confirm occupancy < 3 of 4 years with both tails populated OR `n_confirm_valid < 25` → UNTESTABLE-confirm. Survivorship warning (`events_no_price_data` fraction > 0.10, ADV-02) → verdict flagged **SUSPECT**, not promoted to CONFIRMED until resolved.

## 6. DE-CLUSTERING / PSEUDO-REPLICATION RULE (frozen)

**Setting (frozen):** harness same-ticker de-clustering **ON** — `dedup_same_ticker = True`, **`dedup_window_days = 30`** (30 **calendar** days). **Provenance:** R-1 originally froze `dedup_window_days = 21` calendar days, then amended it **pre-outcome** (legal under R-1's §10, John-approved 2026-06-06) to **30 calendar days**, because 21 **business** days ≈ **29 calendar** days, so the original 21-calendar-day value would let same-stock events **22–28 days apart double-count** (they fall inside the same trailing dose window yet outside the collapse window). R-1b states **30 calendar days directly**, with this provenance noted. Within the dose-aggregation window a company is, by construction, one buying wave and must be one degree of freedom — otherwise overlapping trailing windows on the same ticker would double-count the same dose. 30 calendar days is the conservative collapse aligned to the 21-business-day dose window.

**What it does (`_dedup_events`):** same-ticker `EventRecord`s whose **event_ts ET dates** fall within 30 calendar days collapse to the **first (chronologically earliest)** event — the one carrying the dose computed over its trailing window. One buying wave → one degree of freedom.

**Reporting (binding):** the **raw pre-dedup event count** and **number de-clustered** are recorded in `meta["survivorship"]` (`events_total`, `events_after_dedup`, `events_declustered`) and reported. The bootstrap, quintiling, and all hypotheses are computed on the **de-duplicated** stream only.

**Non-overlapping cross-check (descriptive, not gating):** `use_non_overlapping = False` for the primary (the block bootstrap corrects return overlap); the harness's greedy non-overlapping filter count at 63td is reported descriptively (`meta["non_overlapping"]`).
